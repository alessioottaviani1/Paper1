Each arbitrage is defined by a basis that no-arbitrage forces toward a
well-defined level, and each trade is a fictional fund, in the design of
\citet{duarte2007risk}, that opens when the basis crosses an entry threshold
and closes on reversion or maturity.
% SOURCE: sec3 opening + Duarte fund design.

\emph{BTP~Italia.} The BTP~Italia is indexed to Italian inflation and differs
from conventional linkers in two ways that matter here. It pays the inflation
revaluation with each semiannual coupon and redeems at par, so the notional at
risk never balloons and the two legs of the basis trade carry identical
sovereign credit exposure; and it is placed primarily to retail households,
with a dedicated retail-only auction phase and a loyalty bonus for
buy-and-hold investors. The basis is the fixed-rate-equivalent yield of the
BTP~Italia (converted with zero-coupon inflation swaps, following the
replication logic of \citealp{fleckenstein2014tips}) minus the yield of the
maturity-matched nominal BTP; both legs fund at the same general-collateral
repo rate.
% SOURCE: sec3 BTP Italia paragraphs; ballooning-risk detail -> Appendix.

\emph{CDS--bond basis.} For each issuer in the on-the-run iTraxx Main
universe, the basis is the CDS spread minus the bond z-spread. When negative,
buying the bond and buying protection locks in the gap and returns par in
default, since the CDS tenor matches the bond's residual maturity; we trade
the negative basis only, because the reverse trade requires a corporate
reverse repo subject to recall risk in stress \citep{bai2019cds}.
% SOURCE: sec3 CDS-bond paragraphs.

\emph{iTraxx skew.} The skew is the traded index spread minus the intrinsic
spread bootstrapped from the constituents' CDS curves---name-by-name survival
curves under a flat hazard and 40\% recovery, following
\citet{o2008modelling}; we trade it across the
four iTraxx sub-indices, on-the-run only, both legs under a single netting
set. It is a derivative-on-derivative trade with no cash leg to finance.
% SOURCE: sec3 iTraxx paragraphs.

Individual trades aggregate into signal-weighted indices in the
dislocation-weighting logic of \citet{rebonato2021convexity}: the month-$t$
index return of strategy $i$ is
\begin{equation}
r^{\mathrm{SW}}_{i,t}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_t} w_{k,t}\, r_{k,t},\qquad
w_{k,t}=\frac{\lvert b_{k,0}-\theta_{t_k}\rvert}
{\sum_{j=1}^{K_t}\lvert b_{j,0}-\theta_{t_j}\rvert},
\end{equation}
where $K_t$ counts the trades open in month $t$, $b_{k,0}$ is trade $k$'s
basis at entry, and $\theta_{t_k}$ its expanding-window historical mean, so
that larger dislocations earn larger weights; equal weights $w_{k,t}=1/K_t$
give the equal-weighted counterpart (Appendix~\ref{app:construction}). Transaction costs are charged per trade in
proportion to DV01, at fee tiers that rise with the level of the iTraxx Main
5-year spread---LOW below 60~bps, MEDIUM to 100~bps, HIGH above---so costs are
most conservative exactly when arbitrage returns are largest. All results are
net of these costs.
% SOURCE: sec3 index construction; SW/EW comparison demoted per author decision.

The rules are mechanical and disciplined: a BTP~Italia long, for instance,
opens when the basis exceeds 40~bps and closes when it reverts below 10~bps
(Appendix~\ref{app:construction} tabulates every threshold), and the
overwhelming majority of positions---92.3\% for BTP~Italia, 90.7\% for the
CDS--bond basis, 97.1\% for the iTraxx skew---exit at their reversion target
rather than at maturity or sample end, after mean holding periods of roughly
thirteen, eight, and seven months.
% SOURCE: Tables A.I-A.II (thresholds; target-exit shares; durations 386/240/225 days).
The three Sharpe ratios sit between 0.98 and 1.56 with positive skewness
throughout, and the manipulation-proof measure of
\citet{goetzmann2007portfolio}---reported at risk aversion $\rho=3$ and nearly
invariant to $\rho$---confirms that the risk-adjusted performance is not
manufactured by the dynamic trading rule or by tail risk. Drawdowns are
shallow and short (Figure~\ref{fig:equity}): the deepest, 4.4\% for the
CDS--bond basis in 2008--09, is
an order of magnitude smaller than the strategy's cumulative return over the
sample.
